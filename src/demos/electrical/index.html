Do You Have an Obsolete or Recalled Electrical Panel?

Millions of older American homes still operate on obsolete breaker panels that fail to trip during short circuits, leading directly to structure fires and insurance cancellations.

Extreme Fire Hazard

Federal Pacific Electric (FPE)

Known for 'Stab-Lok' breakers. Studies demonstrate that up to 60% of FPE breakers fail to trip during an overload, melting wires inside walls.

  • Major cause of home electrical fires
  • Frequently denied home insurance coverage
  • Urgent replacement recommended
Melting Bus Bar Defect

Zinsco / Sylvania Panels

Popular from the 1970s. The aluminum interior bus bars corrode and expand, causing breakers to fuse directly to the bar and fail to shut off power.

  • Breakers melt and cannot be switched off
  • Ongoing arcing creates invisible fire hazard
  • Non-compliant with modern NEC codes
Overheating Stabs Hazard

Challenger & Fuse Boxes

1980s Challenger panels feature high failure rates on 15A and 20A single-pole breakers, generating extreme heat and melting surrounding plastic.

  • Severe thermal scorching on bus connections
  • 30A-60A screw-in fuses cannot support appliances
  • Upgrade to Square D 200A recommended

Frequently Asked Electrical Questions

Get clear, expert answers on 200-Amp panel heavy-ups, EV charging capacity, circuit breaker tripping, and electrical permitting.

If your current panel is 100A or 125A and you are adding an EV Level 2 charger, heat pump HVAC, electric tankless water heater, or hot tub, you will exceed safe capacity. Signs of an overloaded panel include flickering lights when the AC kicks on, frequent breaker trips, or a lack of physical breaker slots.

Level 2 EV chargers require a dedicated 240-volt circuit drawing 40 to 60 Amps. During our on-site load assessment, we inspect your service panel capacity, meter base, and existing continuous load. If your panel has adequate headroom, we run dedicated conduit directly to your garage.

Repeated breaker tripping indicates either circuit overload (too many high-draw appliances running simultaneously), a short circuit (hot wire touching neutral), or a ground fault. If a breaker trips immediately after resetting, do not force it—call an electrician immediately to prevent wire damage.

If you see sparks, smoke, or smell burning plastic, safely shut off the main breaker at the top of your panel if it is safe to approach. If there is active fire, evacuate immediately and call 911. Then contact our 24/7 hotline at +1 (412) 796-3968 for emergency technician dispatch.

A standby generator connects directly to your home’s electrical panel via an Automatic Transfer Switch (ATS) and connects to natural gas or liquid propane. When utility power cuts out, the ATS detects the loss and automatically starts the generator within 7 to 10 seconds without manual intervention.

Yes. All panel replacements, generator installs, EV circuits, and commercial buildouts are legally permitted through local municipal building authorities. We handle all paperwork, coordinate power disconnects with the electric utility, and attend the final inspector sign-off.
